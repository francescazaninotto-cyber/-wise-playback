Case Study 01 · Generative Research · Welltech / Yoga Go, 2025

Yoga Go:
Why Users Quit Before They Even Start

A mixed-methods study with 54 churned users, combining AI-conducted interviews via Heard, in-depth 1:1s, and product analytics, to understand what drives disengagement and what it takes to bring people back.

Generative Mixed Methods 54 Participants Shipped +18% Onboarding +12% 30-day Retention

The Problem

A 50M-download app losing users faster than it could acquire them.

Yoga Go had strong top-of-funnel performance but chronic early churn. The product team had quantitative signals but no behavioural explanation for why.

  • Director of Product: redesign onboarding + build social features
  • No prior qual research to validate either assumption
  • Hypothesis: real problems were elsewhere
  • Proposed to research before building

My role: sole researcher, full research lifecycle: from brief definition and study design through to fieldwork, analysis, and stakeholder readout.

Methodology

Why mixed methods?

Quantitative data told us when users left. Qualitative research told us why. I designed a study to bring both together.

Analytics first

I started with product analytics to identify three user groups based on actual engagement: Ghost, Casual, and Power Users. That shaped who we recruited.

Segmentation

We focused on Casual (split into Low and High engaged) because they were by far the largest group, and on Power Users as a control to understand what retention looked like when it worked. Ghost users were deprioritised, as they were too early in the funnel to yield actionable insights within this sprint.

AI Interviews · Heard

48 interviews via Heard, run in parallel with the 1:1s. Covered all three segments at scale and helped surface the main themes.

In-depth 1:1 Interviews

6 interviews, a mix across groups, run in the same window as the AI interviews. These went deeper: screen sharing, open exploration.

Week 1

Segmentation and recruitment

Research brief co-created with PM and Data leads. Segments defined from analytics. Screener built to match real behavioural patterns. Email campaign launched to recruit participants. PM and designers were invited to observe interviews throughout the study.

Week 2–3

AI interviews + 1:1s run in parallel

48 Heard interviews and 6 in-depth 1:1s ran across two weeks. We had issues with Heard early on: some participants hadn't completed all questions but had been included and incentivised. The 1:1s included screen sharing: participants walked through the app live, which surfaced navigation issues we hadn't anticipated.

Week 4

Analysis

Thematic analysis across both interview sets. Coded for motivation type, churn moment, segment behaviour, and expectation gaps.

Week 5

Triangulation + readout

One day with the data team to check qual findings against behavioural data. Then a cross-functional readout with PM, Design, Lifecycle, and Data. Findings ranked by impact, owners and timelines assigned.

Week 5 was not the end. After the readout I ran workshops to present the data to wider teams, particularly Marketing. I also transformed the report into a podcast and shared it across teams via Slack.

Participants

Three groups, three different reasons for leaving.

User problems identified

Segmenting by how far users got before churning showed us that different groups were failing for completely different reasons, and needed completely different solutions.

🏃

Group 1 · Low-Engaged Casual

Used once or twice, then left. They got confused during onboarding and left before they'd even seen what the app could do.

🔄

Group 2 · High-Engaged Casual

Used the app several times but lost momentum. No progress feedback, no reminders, nothing to pull them back after a break.

💪

Group 3 · Power Users

Committed users who hit a ceiling. The content stopped feeling new, and there was nothing to grow into. They left even though they'd invested.

The over-40 segment (33/54 participants) drove the most actionable insights. Holistic wellness goals, first-time digital fitness users, needed guidance the app didn't offer.

Age split across all segments

21/54
Under 40

Goal-driven, variety-seeking, quick to switch apps. Motivated by fast results, they disengaged when the experience felt generic.

33/54
Over 40

Managing real physical needs: mobility, joint health, stress. Often using a fitness app for the first time. Needed structured guidance and felt let down when it wasn't there.

How we ran the 1:1 interviews

Participants showed us the app live.

In the 1:1s, participants shared their screens and navigated the app while talking. After free navigation, we showed Figma prototypes to explore design directions.

Key Findings

Four reasons users left.

🎯

1. The personalisation gap

Users answered an onboarding quiz but saw no difference in their recommendations. The promise of a personalised app delivered a generic experience. 42/54 users raised this.

🧭

2. Navigation friction killed momentum

Users couldn't find missed workouts, restart plans, or understand the navigation logic. Confusion after onboarding led to abandonment before value was delivered. 34/54 users struggled.

🔔

3. No motivation loop

No reminders, no streak tracking, no feedback on progress. Without anything pulling them back, users simply forgot to return. 36/54 users cited this.

📦

4. Content felt repetitive

After a few weeks, workouts felt identical. Power users hit a ceiling. Casual users got bored before building a habit. 30/54 users wanted more variety.

Behavioural Science Layer

Users weren't just churning. They were disappointed.

Reframing the findings as expectation gaps changed how the product team responded.

The green text in each card is my recommendation to the team, mapped to the gap identified.

Marketing / product gap

What the ads promised and what the app delivered weren't the same thing

  • Audit what the ads and App Store claim against what users actually find in week one
  • Look at retention by campaign to see which messages cause the most drop-off
Over-40 / usability gap

This group needed guidance the app didn't give them

  • Clearer exercise explanations, step-by-step onboarding, help when they get stuck
  • Test whether this improves completing the first workout and coming back at day 7 and 30
Power users / re-entry gap

They wanted to come back. The app made it hard

  • Spot when an engaged user goes quiet and reach out before they fully leave
  • Acknowledge the gap, offer something easy to do first, help them feel like they never lost their progress

So what changed

Once we framed it as expectation gaps, the team stopped asking "what's broken?" and started asking "what did we promise?"

  • Casual users needed the product to deliver what marketing said it would
  • Over-40 users needed a hand getting started, not just a better UI
  • Power users needed a way back in, not a new user flow

Impact · Nikki Anderson Framework

Three types of research success.

What changed because of this research, across product, strategy, and how the team works.

1 · Strategic Influence

Research changed the roadmap

  • Original brief: social features + onboarding redesign
  • Research reframed priorities: habit formation, content variety, personalisation
  • Over-40 users: first dedicated research strand
  • Lifecycle Marketing: new push strategy by segment. +17% WAU
  • Re-entry flow added to roadmap

2 · Direct Product Impact

Features shipped from findings

+18%
Onboarding completion Quiz results now visibly connected to what users see next.
+12%
30-day retention Streak tracking, progress feedback, and a re-entry flow added.
+15%
Paid conversion The moment users were asked to upgrade was redesigned around their goals, not a countdown timer.

3 · Operational / ResOps Impact

From one study to a scaled research programme

  • Built a programme to enable designers to run their own interviews using Heard
  • Documentation suite: guide writing, question design, synthesis
  • Scale research capacity without growing the team

Reflection

What I'd do differently.

  • Over-40 segment larger and more complex than expected: deserved its own dedicated study
  • More 1:1s per segment for richer group-level depth
  • First time using Heard: early on, participants who didn't complete all questions were still included in analysis and incentivised. Changed this mid-study to improve data quality: a lesson in setting quality thresholds before fieldwork begins

In summary

In five weeks, with no prior qual research on these users, we spoke to 54 churned users, explained why they left, and gave the team a roadmap to bring them back.

The team shifted from proposing solutions to asking better questions.
